The Milky Way

Meet our home galaxy, the Milky Way! Its name describes the hazy band of light we can see in the night sky looking up from Earth. When we take a closer look, we find a barred spiral galaxy with a visible diameter of 150,000 to 200,000 light-years, containing 100 to 400 billion stars of all different kinds.

Like early explorers mapping the continents of our globe, astronomers are busy charting the spiral structure of our galaxy. Using infrared images from NASA's Spitzer Space Telescope, scientists discovered that the Milky Way's elegant spiral structure is dominated by just two major arms—Scutum–Centaurus and Perseus—wrapping off the ends of a central bar of stars. Previously, our galaxy was thought to possess four major arms. The two now-demoted minor arms (Norma and Sagittarius) are less distinct and located between the major arms. The major arms consist of the highest densities of both young and old stars, while the minor arms are primarily filled with gas and pockets of star-forming activity. Our Sun lies near a small, partial arm called the Orion Arm, or Orion Spur, located between the Sagittarius and Perseus arms.

Top Sights to See

If you want to travel our beautiful galaxy, you shouldn’t miss out on these stunning places.

1The Skyline

The skyline of the Milky Way has some amazing sights to offer. The gravitational pull of the other galaxies around it causes its slight curve. The gigantic bubbles above and below the Milky Way can be seen in high-energy gamma rays and X-rays and are probably caused by the Supermassive Black Hole in the center of the galaxy.

2Possible Nightlife Hotspots

All over the Milky Way, there are an estimated number of about one billion Earth-sized exoplanets with properties that could potentially sustain life as we know it. One of the most interesting exoplanets is Kepler-452b – a total of 1,402 light-years away. It has the perfect distance to its star, making it not too hot and not too cold. One orbit around its star takes 385 days, only a tiny bit longer than Earth needs to travel around the Sun – ideal for a night out!

3Industrial District

Nebulae are huge clouds of dust and gas that act as galactic nurseries: new stars are constantly born here. When some of these hot young stars explode in supernovae, they release stellar winds and ultraviolet radiation, forming the clouds into fantastic landscapes. One pretty example is called the Pillars of Creation, a part of the Eagle Nebula. In 2007 NASA reported that the cloud had been destroyed by a supernova – luckily this turned out to be wrong and you can still enjoy the view of this showstopper.

4The City Center

Right in the middle of the Milky Way sits Sagittarius A*, our galaxy’s central Supermassive Black Hole, estimated to be four million times heavier than our Sun. For something so massive, it is pretty good at hiding. But the fact that some stars were orbiting unusually fast around what appeared to be nothing gave it away once and for all. A must-see!

5Weekend Escapes

Many star clusters are located around the halo of the Milky Way. We recommend visiting the globular cluster NGC 2419. It is also called the Intergalactic Wanderer because with its three-billion-year orbit it travels further out than the Milky Way’s neighbor galaxy, the Large Magellanic Cloud. This is where it currently resides. So right now it is located pretty far away from the center of the Milky Way. But it is definitely worth a day trip!
